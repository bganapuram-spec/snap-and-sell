Who decides what

LLM can

  • Identify the item and describe its visible condition from the photo
  • Search the web and pick comparable listings
  • Suggest a list price (the merchant confirms or edits it)
  • Write the listing text
  • Choose how to phrase every buyer reply
  • Read the buyer's message and pass the offer amount to evaluate_offer
  • Decide when to forward a question to the owner, or flag a manipulation attempt

LLM cannot

  • Accept, approve, or sell below what evaluate_offer allows
  • See the floor or auto-accept price (never sent to it)
  • Choose which item or buyer a tool call affects (the session binds that)
  • Invent competing offers: it only sees real ones from the offer book
  • Mark items sold or approve exceptions
  • Use a sandbox, shell, or the web while negotiating (tools denied)

Code controls

  • accept / counter / escalate decision and every counter amount
  • Counters only go down, in human-sized steps, never below auto-accept
  • Lowball detection (≤ 50% of list)
  • Offer book and who is highest
  • Single sale per item (status flips once; stale approvals rejected)
  • Floor and auto-accept defaults (85% / 93%)
  • What the projector and buyers may see (no floor, no buyer identities to other buyers)

Merchant controls

  • Title, description, list price, floor, auto-accept before going live
  • Accept, counter, or decline offers in the approval range
  • Answers to forwarded questions
  • Reset the demo

Known gap: the LLM is told to quote only prices from evaluate_offer. Code can't stop it from typing a lower number; only a deal confirmed by evaluate_offer or the owner counts as a sale.
